Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy BMI?
A BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 is considered normal. Below 18.5 is underweight, 25–29.9 is overweight, and 30+ is obese.
Is BMI accurate for everyone?
No. Athletes with high muscle mass may show a high BMI despite low body fat. It is a quick screening measure, not a full health assessment.
How are the diet calories calculated?
We first estimate your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation: 10×weight(kg) + 6.25×height(cm) − 5×age + 5 (men) or −161 (women). Multiplying BMR by your activity level gives your maintenance calories (TDEE). The target is then adjusted by your BMI category: +300 kcal to gain, 0 to maintain, and −400/−500 kcal to lose.
How is the macro and nutrient split worked out?
Protein is set at 1.6–2.0 g per kg of body weight (higher when cutting to preserve muscle). Fat is 25% of target calories, and carbohydrates fill the remainder. Fiber is ~14 g per 1000 kcal, water ~35 ml per kg body weight, added sugar is capped at 5% of energy, and saturated fat at 10%.
